# Meeting script — Jeremy Williams

30 minutes. Demo first, then the decision. Print this and [demo-script.md](./demo-script.md). The demo file is the middle of the meeting; this file is the open and the ask.

One sentence he should be able to repeat: "DownUnder tells a woman whether a medicine and her birth control affect each other, and what adverse effects the label reports for her body. She uses it free. A campus can buy it. The next money is a research grant after Sandai clears the company."

## What you are asking him to do

You are not asking for a seed round, and you are not asking him to introduce investors. Seed is early 2028, after a pilot is written up and a second campus or a paid year is committed. Venture funds that need a return in a year will pass, and they should.

You are asking him to pick one of these. Lead with the first.

**A. Sandai sponsors the next gate, and he makes one introduction.** Through February 2027 that means your time, a lawyer for the entity and IP memo, a stipend for one pharmacist or campus nurse practitioner, and an introduction to that person. If he asks what the whole pre-grant stretch costs, the plan’s range through July 2027 is $300k–$600k in cash and in-kind (advisor, counsel, one contract engineer, pharmacist labeling, hosting). Do not open with that number. If he wants a February budget, tell him you will send it within a week. Do not invent a figure in the room.

**B. He will not fund operating costs.** Then the only acceptable no is a yes to the legal question plus two introductions: the clinician, and someone who has submitted an NIH small-business grant. Without a sponsor, the August 2027 pilot slips a full year, and you say that out loud.

The best outcome is A, with the grant as the second check, not the first.

## 0:00 — Open (about 2 minutes)

"I want to show you the demo, then I want a decision on how the next four months get paid for. You were right to point at SBIR. I looked up the dates. The next deadline is January 5, 2027, and a project submitted that day cannot start before July 2027. So the grant cannot pay for this winter. Someone has to."

Then start [demo-script.md](./demo-script.md) at beat 1. Stop after beat 8 unless he asks to keep going. That is Maya, rifampin, lamotrigine, amoxicillin, the unresolved product, the IUD contrast, Dr. Reyes, and the dashboard including the label-extraction button.

Volunteer, before he asks: "The engine behind this demo is real for those cases. The gold set is 50 items with zero dangerous misses. It is not the 150 to 300 a pharmacist still has to label. That labeling is the next gate."

If he interrupts, answer in one sentence and return to the screen. Likely interrupts are in the objection list below.

## On the clinician screen and the dashboard — why a health center buys them

Say this while those two screens are up. Do not save it for the money section. The prices mean nothing until he knows what they are paying for.

**Clinician, on `/clinic`.** "The clinician is the nurse practitioner in student health, not a general university nurse and not the director. She sees twenty or thirty students a day. The questions she currently answers from memory are specific. Does this antibiotic cancel the pill? I started sertraline, does that change anything? I was prescribed something that says I need reliable birth control — is a pill enough, or is my IUD already enough? Rifampin, a seizure drug, St John's wort. Today that answer comes from what she remembers, from Lexicomp, or from a call to the pharmacy.

She only sees checks the student agreed to share. On each one she has the result, the adverse effects from the label, and the passage. She marks confirmed, dismissed, or advised. She uses that in three ways: she shows it to the student in the room instead of reconstructing the label, she spends the visit on the decision instead of the lookup, and the mark is the record that a professional reviewed it. It does not write itself into the campus medical record. That export is not built. Students never see this screen. The login that would sit in front of it is not built either."

**Director, on `/admin`.** "The buyer is the student health director. She does not counsel the student and she does not open this to find Maya. She gets counts for her own campus. How many checks. How many known interactions. How many possible. How many where our sources had nothing. How often we had to say we could not tell. Anything under ten is hidden.

What she can do with that: see whether students are using it at all. See whether the load is real interactions her nurse practitioners should act on, or a high abstention rate that means a pharmacist is still getting the hard cases. Take those counts into a budget meeting and into a quality write-up. She cannot see a name, a medicine list, or a single student's result. Say, before he reads the figures, that the numbers on this demo screen are a sample campus, not a live tally of the clicks you just made."

If he asks why the student is free and the clinic pays: "The student will not pay, and we will not put the check behind a subscription. The health center pays because the worklist and the counts belong to them. The free app is how the question arrives already sourced, instead of as another interruption for the nurse practitioner."

## If he wants revenue as soon as possible

Say the calendar out loud. Do not offer a sooner customer date than this.

"The first customer dollar I can honestly put on a date is August 2027. That is a pilot fee, up to about $5,000, and only if the student health director signs by June. Campuses set the budget and the clinic calendar around the fall semester. If we miss June, that dollar moves to August 2028.

The first subscriptions are August 2028. About $6,000 to $30,000 per campus per year, paid by the student health director for the worklist and the counts. Five to ten campuses is about $100,000. A paid second year for the pilot campus can start after the fall write-up, which is January 2028 at the earliest, if she converts instead of waiting for the next August.

Nothing a student health center pays arrives this winter. Sandai or a grant covers November through July. Compressing the product does not move a university's buying cycle."

## After the demo — the money (about 10 minutes)

Say this, then stop.

"Three different people pay for three different things.

First, Sandai, now through July 2027. That buys the proof a grant and a campus will ask for: a pharmacist advisor, the larger labeled set, a one-page regulatory memo, and a company that is allowed to hold a grant. No campus revenue required.

Second, an NIH small-business grant. Phase I is on the order of a few hundred thousand dollars, for the science, not for equity. I am not applying to the contraception branch just because the word matches. Their published topics are new contraceptive methods, not a checker. The fit to confirm before we write is the National Library of Medicine, which funds information tools, and NICHD’s obstetric and pediatric pharmacology branch, which explicitly lists AI decision-support tools for drug safety in pregnant and lactating women. We email the program officer before we draft. Standard deadlines are January 5, April 5, and September 5. Review takes months. Phase II is the larger follow-on, and only if Phase I works.

Third, a student health center. First possible payment is a pilot fee of up to about $5,000 in August 2027, if the director signs by June. The subscription, $6,000 to $30,000 a year for the nurse practitioner's worklist and the director's counts, starts August 2028. Five to ten campuses is about $100,000. A pilot campus can convert as early as January 2028 if she does not wait for the next fall. Investors see it after that, not before.

Campus health centers alone are about a $27 million a year market. I am not inflating that. If you add telehealth, pharmacies, insurers, and the later health modules, the spending in that whole category is an estimate around $180–250 million a year. That is the size of the pond. Our 2030 sales, if the wedge works, are the slice we can actually win: about 40 campuses, two telehealth partners, one pharmacy contract, and one early insurer, around $1.5–2 million a year. We do not need the whole pond to be ours this decade."

Then the question, verbatim:

"What I need from you today is a choice. Will Sandai cover the gate through February — my time, counsel on the company and the IP, a pharmacist stipend, and an introduction to that person — or will you not put money in, and instead introduce the clinician and a grants person? What would you need to see to say yes to the first one?"

Count to five. Write down his words.

### If he leans toward paying

Agree the pieces, not a round. Repeat them back: time, counsel, advisor stipend, one intro. Ask who else at Sandai has to be comfortable (finance, whoever owns your employment agreement). Offer the July range only if he asks what happens after February. End with a date: "I will send the February budget and a one-page IP question by [day]. Can we look at it on [date]?"

### If he leans toward introductions only

Take them. Ask for the names before you leave. Then say: "Without a sponsor, January’s grant is the first money, and it cannot start until July, so the August pilot moves to 2028. I will still do that if it is the path. I need you to say that is the path."

Ask him for the grants person by name, and for permission to use his name in the clinician email.

### If he wants both

That is the plan. Sandai carries the winter. The January 5 application is the science check, written only after counsel says the company is eligible. He does not have to meet NIH. He has to decide the company.

### If he says "go raise it"

"From whom, this quarter? A seed fund will ask for pilot revenue we will not have until 2028. The buyer of this stage is a research agency, and they require a small business that is majority-owned by individuals. If Sandai owns the company outright, we may be ineligible. That is the legal question in the February budget. Venture introductions are useful after the pilot, and I will ask you for them then."

## The eligibility point (say it even if he does not)

"SBIR is a US for-profit small business, majority-owned by people or other small businesses, with a principal investigator who can commit real time. STTR is the version where a university lab does part of the work, which fits if the pharmacist is at a campus. Your ownership of this project can block the grant. I would rather hear that now than in a rejection. I am also not assuming my Sandai employment agreement already gives me the IP. Counsel should say whose invention this is before anyone applies."

## Objections, one sentence each

- "Is this just a drug checker?" — "A checker asks if two drugs interact. We ask if her specific method still works, if anything she takes means she needs reliable contraception, and what adverse effects the label reports for her body even when birth control is unaffected. The IUD-versus-pill screen is the contraception difference. The effect list under it is the rest."
- "Is the market big enough?" — "Campus is about $27 million a year. That is the wedge, not the company. I will not show you a billion-dollar TAM."
- "What is the AI?" — "One job: pull a contraception sentence out of a public label and quote it. It cannot override a guideline and it never sees her chart. Calling the product predictive would cost us the clinicians."
- "Liability, and Sandai’s name." — "We never say safe. Every result cites a source. The patient view is information, the clinician view is where a professional decides. Whether Sandai’s name is on the product is your call, and it can wait until the regulatory memo."
- "Are you leaving your job to do this?" — "Not as a condition of today. The February ask includes how much of my time this takes. You tell me what Sandai can spare."
- "Students will not use it." — "The buyer is the health center, for the worklist and the counts. Student use is how the question arrives already sourced. The pilot will count repeat use and we will publish it either way."
- "Why would a clinic pay if the app is free?" — "The student health director is not paying for the student's screen. She is paying so her nurse practitioner can review shared checks with the source attached, and so she gets campus counts with no names."
- "I need revenue now." — "The first customer dollar is August 2027, a pilot fee of up to about $5,000, if they sign by June. Subscriptions are August 2028. A university will not buy this in November."
- "Why not apply to every department I sent you?" — "Those sites fund different science. Contraception-branch SBIR wants new methods. Our project is a safety check. I will confirm NLM and NICHD pharmacology with a program officer before we write, instead of spraying the list."

## What you do not ask for today

- A $2–3.5 million seed, or a Series A conversation.
- Introductions to health-tech funds.
- Permission to put Sandai’s brand on the student app.
- A yes to reminders, Spanish, an EHR integration, or a live model. Those are on the Coming screen and stay there.
- His advice on training a model on student records. That is out. Aggregates, later, only with consent and small cells hidden.

## Last two minutes

Summarize what you heard. "So the path you want is [A or B], and the person you will introduce is [name]."

Then: "I will email tonight with those three lines, the day I will send the February note, and the day we look at it. What would you need to see in four weeks?"

Write the answer down. That list is the February deliverable, not a new feature.
