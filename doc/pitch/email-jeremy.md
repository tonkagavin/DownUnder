# Email to Jeremy Williams

Subject: DownUnder — would you look at this with me?

Jeremy,

DownUnder tells a woman whether a medicine and her birth control affect each other, and whether the label describes an adverse effect because she is on that method. The rest of the label is on the medicine in her profile, not on the check. It cites the passage. It says "ask a pharmacist" when it cannot tell. It never says "safe." Students use it free.

A student health center pays for two screens she never sees.

The clinician is the nurse practitioner in campus health. In the visit, students ask her from memory: "Does this antibiotic cancel my pill?" "I started an antidepressant — does that matter?" "This drug says I need birth control. Is my IUD enough?" She gets a worklist of the checks that student agreed to share, with the source on the page, and she marks that she confirmed it, dismissed it, or advised her. She uses that in the room instead of calling the pharmacy.

The person who signs is the student health director. She gets counts for her campus only: how many checks, how many were known interactions, how often we could not tell. Anything under ten is hidden, so she never sees a name. She uses those counts to see whether students are asking, whether the tool is flagging cases her staff should act on or sending too many onward, and to justify next year's budget.

The earliest customer payment is a pilot fee of up to about $5,000 in August 2027, if a campus signs by June. The first subscriptions are August 2028, about $6,000 to $30,000 per campus per year. Five to ten campuses is about $100,000.

That 2028 product is still one question: her birth control and her medicines. It becomes a platform only after that is selling. In 2029 the same profile and the same engine take a second question, hormonal conditions or pregnancy and breastfeeding, without a rebuild, and an outside clinic or telehealth company can run the check through an API. She keeps the record after graduation. From 2030 the same record covers menopause, and the buyers add pharmacies, employers, and health plans. If every one of those buyers paid for every stage, that category is an estimated $180–250 million a year. That is the size of the market, not our sales. The piece we could actually hold by 2030 is about 40 campuses, two telehealth partners, one pharmacy contract, and one early insurer: around $1.5–2 million a year.

Do you think this is a viable product? If you want to hear more, or see the demo I have, when are you free?

Gavin
